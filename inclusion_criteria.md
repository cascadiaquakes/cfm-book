# Inclusion Criteria

*From the CRESCENT CFM ReadMe, which will be published on Zenodo with CFM v1.0.*

For CFM v1.0, we primarily focus on upper plate faults both onshore and offshore of the Cascadia margin. A few notable lower plate faults in the offshore region are included. Upper plate faults that lie fully or mostly within southern British Columbia, Washington, Oregon, and northern California between the Mendocino Triple Junction and the northern end of Vancouver Island are considered for inclusion. We partly rely on political boundaries for fault inclusion although these do not represent geologic domain boundaries. Faults that lie within this region are evaluated for inclusion based on three components:

1. **Evidence of activity.** Faults must fall into one of the three activity levels:
   - **Level 1:** Evidence for nucleating and hosting slip during an earthquake, either single or multi-segment since the Late Pleistocene (<130 ka) (*e.g., Gales Creek fault, OR*).
   - **Level 2:** Evidence for hosting slip during a multi-segment earthquake since the Late Pleistocene (<130 ka) but not likely to nucleate slip during a single-segment earthquake (*e.g., accretionary wedge splay faults*).
   - **Level 3:** Lacking evidence for recent fault slip and likely inactive since the Late Pleistocene (<130 ka) but could become active in the current stress conditions. Participation in single or multi-segment earthquakes is unknown (*e.g., Straight Creek fault, WA, or bedrock faults without evidence of offset in Late Pleistocene and younger (<130 ka) deposits, evaluated on a case-by-case basis*).
2. **Length.**
   - Greater than or equal to 7 km.
   - Faults shorter than this may be evaluated on a case-by-case basis for inclusion (e.g., if it is identified as a key fault in a larger system).
3. **Published evidence.** Evidence for activity and geometry must be available in a citable, publicly available product (e.g., peer-reviewed journal, state geological survey report, published consulting report) that has undergone some level of peer review.

Faults included in CFM v1.0 also need to have sufficient characterization in their sources to generate a 3D model. At a minimum, faults need to have a documented mapped trace, dip direction, and sense of slip. We use these characterizations to assign default values for dip magnitude and generate a 3D model. The inclusion of Level 3 faults relied heavily on the community review process, is incomplete, and will improve with future versions of the CFM.
